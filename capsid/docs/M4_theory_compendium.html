0. What this document is, and what it is not

Module 4 answers one question: given a pool of MS2 coat-protein dimers, what fraction closes into well-formed T=3 capsids, and at what dimer concentration is that fraction maximal?

That question has been attacked at four different levels of description, each of which exists in this project as running code rather than as prose. This compendium is the theory behind all four, assembled as one argument:

Layer Level of description State variable Answers
L1 Deterministic rate equations Concentration of each partial shell size Yield vs concentration and time
L2 Classical nucleation theory Cluster size as a continuous coordinate Where in parameter space assembly is possible at all
L3 Coarse-grained Monte Carlo Positions and orientations of 90 subunits The geometry L1 must assume
L4 Stochastic co-assembly with cargo Occupancy of each face plus a threading path How packaging signals change the answer

This document is not a summary of the four existing Module 4 documents. It is the derivation chain, written once, with the layers' points of contact and their one published disagreement made explicit. §9 is the wet-lab contract: which measurement enters, which parameter it moves, and what the model is obliged to do when the number disagrees.

Each layer, the bridge between them and the fitting routine ship as runnable notebooks (M4_L1_rate_equations.ipynb, M4_L2_nucleation_phase_diagram.ipynb, M4_L3_coarsegrained_MC.ipynb, M4_L4_packaging_signal_coassembly.ipynb, M4_bridge_CG_to_ODE_to_bench.ipynb, M4_FIT_to_bench_data.ipynb). Each writes out the module it needs and runs top to bottom on a fresh machine; see their README.md. The experimental designs, literature landscape and Spanish-language team material live in their own artifacts and are referenced, not repeated.

Every equation below is either derived here from a stated physical assumption or lifted verbatim from a cited paper. Where a number appears, it was read from the code that produces it, not from memory — the project rule is that no value reaches a deliverable without resolving against its source.

1. Notation and the constants shared by all four layers

Throughout, a subunit means one coat-protein dimer, the obligate structural unit of MS2 established crystallographically by Golmohammadi and colleagues and refined for recombinant assembly by Ni and colleagues. A T=3 shell contains

Free energies are in kcal/mol and the thermal scale is

with expressed relative to the standard state. This single relation is what lets a measured affinity enter a kinetic model, and it is used in L1, L4 and Module 5 alike.

Two energies recur and must be kept distinct:

  • — the association penalty per subunit added: the translational and rotational entropy a free dimer surrenders on joining a shell. Zlotnick's foundational analysis of icosahedral assembly identifies this term as the origin of the nucleation barrier.
  • — the free energy of one subunit-subunit contact. Ceres and Zlotnick measured it directly for hepatitis B capsids and found it remarkably weak, a few kcal/mol, which is precisely why assembly is reversible enough to be productive.

Assembly is possible because a subunit joining an existing shell forms more than one contact, so a single penalty is paid against several favourable contacts. Everything in Module 4 is an elaboration of that one sentence.

More dimer is not better. The optimum is interior, at µM. The mechanism of the collapse is visible in the columns: between 2.5 and 5 µM the yield falls from 95% to nil while free dimer stays below 1%. The protein has not failed to react — it has all reacted, into partial shells that can no longer find the subunits they need. That is kinetic trapping, and it is the physically interesting content of the layer. Zlotnick and Endres established the mechanism; Hagan and Chandler's simulations connect it to the same rate-equation structure used here.

The existence of an interior optimum is also the model's principal falsifiable claim. A monotonically saturating yield curve would break the detailed-balance construction and both scales built on it.

3. Layer L2 — classical nucleation theory as the widening layer

L1 answers "how much, when" for one parameter set. L2 answers a different question — for which contact energies and concentrations is productive assembly possible at all — by coarse-graining the ladder into a continuous droplet free energy. The gain is a phase diagram; the cost is that discreteness and geometry are lost.

Three sources of evidence cannot all be right. We publish the disagreement rather than picking the flattering branch, because one benchtop concentration sweep adjudicates it: a peak near 8–12 µM vindicates the CG geometry, near 2.5 µM indicts it, and no peak at all falsifies the detailed-balance construction underneath both.

6. Layer L4 — packaging-signal-mediated co-assembly

L1–L3 model protein alone. The BruSafe particle assembles around an mRNA carrying engineered pac-sites, and Dykeman, Stockley and Twarock showed that such signals change assembly qualitatively, not just quantitatively. L4 is an independent re-implementation of their framework on a 12-face study system, validated on exact invariants before any kinetics were compared.

6.1 Two reaction orders, both at detailed balance

Two classes of event occur. A subunit can bind a packaging signal on the cargo — second order, since it consumes free protein — or two adjacent bound subunits can form a protein-protein bond — first order, an intramolecular closure. 6.2 The trap is geometric, not energetic

The mechanism that makes this layer non-trivial: the cargo threads the shell as a self-avoiding walk on the face-adjacency graph. A growing end can reach a face all of whose neighbours are already visited — a dead end. The shell is then incomplete and cannot be finished without reversing, and reversal means paying to break protein bonds. The trap therefore arises from the topology of the threading path, not from an energy barrier. Two earlier versions of this engine were discarded for getting exactly this wrong: one allowed free rerouting and produced near-complete yield in every condition (nothing ever trapped), the other gated escape on an invented reserved-face rule and over-trapped.

6.3 Validation on exact invariants

Before comparing any kinetics, two combinatorial quantities of the published model were reproduced exactly:

  • 73 unique protein-only intermediates,
  • 1,264 distinct threading paths, from 75,840 directed Hamiltonian paths divided by the order-60 icosahedral rotation group.

Two exact agreements on quantities that depend only on the geometry and the assembly rules are the licence to compare rates at all.

8. Limits of validity

Stated plainly, because a model whose limits are hidden cannot be trusted where it does work.

  1. L1 is a mean-field rate equation. It tracks concentrations of size classes, not individual shells, so it cannot distinguish a well-formed 90-mer from a malformed one of the same size. Its yield is properly read as an upper bound on well-formed particles. Hagan's review of assembly modelling makes this limitation explicit for exactly this class of model.
  2. One-subunit-at-a-time growth excludes fusion of partial shells. Justified while intermediates are rare; it is least safe deep in the trapped regime, which is where the model's most dramatic prediction lives.
  3. L2's continuum rim term is wrong at small , where discreteness dominates. Its should be read as a scaling statement, not a prediction of the nucleus.
  4. L3 is a 90-particle model in reduced units, with a patch geometry that approximates a T=3 lattice site rather than reproducing the MS2 interface. Its optimum transfers as a direction, not a molar concentration.
  5. L4 runs on a 12-face study system, not the T=3 lattice. Its ordering and reversals transfer; its magnitudes do not. Porting it to 90 faces is the natural extension.
  6. No electrostatics. Cargo charge, ionic strength and pH enter only implicitly through effective energies, whereas Chevreuil and colleagues show the assembly pathway itself can depend on them.
  7. Temperature enters only through . The hydrophobic, entropy-driven character of the interface means the energies themselves are temperature-dependent; the temperature lever is capped accordingly.
  8. The two false retraction flags in the corpus (10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010652, 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009913) are new_version updates re-verified against Crossref, not retractions, and must be cleared before the corpus is cited as evidence of screening.

9. Wet-lab validation: where this model meets the bench

Everything above is Mode A — prediction with no data in it. This section is the contract between the model and the wet lab: which measurements enter, which parameter each one moves, and what the model is obliged to do when the number comes back wrong. It is written for the equipment actually available: a plate reader, a gel rig, a spectrophotometer, and at most three TEM sessions, which is the binding constraint on the whole design.

9.1 The one experiment that matters most

A concentration sweep of coat-protein dimer, read by turbidity, with TEM at three points.

Every layer of Module 4 makes the same qualitative claim — that yield is non-monotonic in dimer concentration, rising to an optimum and then collapsing into kinetic traps. L1 derives it from rate equations, L2 explains it as a moving barrier, L3 reproduces it from geometry alone. A single sweep tests all three at once. If yield instead saturates monotonically, the central claim of the module is wrong, and no amount of re-fitting rescues it.

Design: assemble at µM (add 8 and 12 µM if protein allows), 240 min, three replicates, one buffer. Read continuously in the plate reader.

9.2 Why turbidity alone is not enough

Turbidity is a mass-squared signal: for particles small against the wavelength, scattering per species goes as (mass), so the plate reader reports

which a partial shell answers almost as loudly as a closed one. A 60-dimer malformed aggregate scatters as much as a complete capsid. This is precisely the kinetic-trap signature: above the optimum the model predicts turbidity that plateaus high while the closed-capsid count collapses to zero. A plate reader on its own cannot tell that apart from success, which is the entire reason TEM sessions must be spent, and spent in the right places.

Native PAGE is the cheap partial fix: closed capsids run as a single sharp species, trapped intermediates as a smear. It resolves the ambiguity qualitatively for every concentration in the sweep, at no TEM cost.

9.3 Spending three TEM sessions

The three sessions are the scarce resource, so they are allocated to the three points where the model's predictions differ most, not spread evenly across the sweep:

Session Model predicts What it discriminates
1 at the turbidity peak mostly closed 27 nm shells confirms the optimum is productive, not just scattering
2 2× above the peak high turbidity, few closed shells, mass in large partial shells the trap prediction — the single most falsifiable claim in the module
3 ~0.4× the peak few particles, most mass free dimer confirms the low side fails by not nucleating, not by trapping

Session 2 is the one that earns the module its keep. Do not sacrifice it to a replicate.

Count ≥ 200 particles per grid and score each as correct-diameter (25–29 nm) or not; the fraction is what the model consumes. Record the count itself — it sets the binomial error bar, and a fraction with no behind it cannot be weighted in a fit.

9.6 What we do if the data disagrees

Stated in advance, so the response is not chosen after seeing the result:

  • Optimum in the wrong place → re-fit . Expected; this is what the fitting notebook is for.
  • No optimum at all, monotonic saturation → L1's mechanism is wrong for MS2. Report it. The nucleation-elongation picture would not survive that, and dressing it up would be worse than losing it.
  • Optimum present but TEM above it shows free dimer rather than partial shells → the yield collapse is real but the mechanism is not trapping. L1 keeps its curve and loses its explanation.
  • Fitted outside the −2.9 to −4.4 kcal/mol literature interval → this has already happened. The bridge inverts to −4.78 kcal/mol, and the notebook prints the comparison as a plain False. It is reported as a disagreement (§5.3), not tuned away.
